You dropped in first — the snow felt perfect, a little wind-buffed but stable. Jordan and Alex followed.

Halfway down the face, you hear the crack. It starts above the rock band, 80 metres wide, and it moves faster than you expect. You dig your pole tip in and hold. The slab hits and rolls past you in a wall of white.

Then it stops. The mountain goes quiet.

You look down. Alex is visible — upper body above the debris, not moving. Jordan is gone. Last seen 30 metres downslope of the release point. The debris field is rough and deep.

Your beacon is switched to search. You have maybe 15 minutes before the survival curve turns steep.
